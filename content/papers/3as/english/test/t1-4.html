Mesopotamia, which means “the land between the rivers”, lay between the Tigris and the Euphrates, mainly in what is now Iraq. Around 3200 BC, the Sumerians, who had settled in the south of this fertile plain, developed one of the first writing systems in the world. They pressed a sharpened reed into soft clay tablets, which left wedge-shaped marks. Historians call this script cuneiform.

At first, writing was used to record goods such as grain and cattle. Later, scribes used it to write letters, stories and laws. Around 1750 BC, the Babylonian king Hammurabi had his famous code of laws carved on a stone pillar. Although the cities of Mesopotamia declined after centuries of wars, their heritage has survived: the 60-minute hour and the 360-degree circle come from their counting system, which was based on 60.
