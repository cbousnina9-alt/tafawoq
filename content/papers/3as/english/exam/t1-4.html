Every morning, millions of people drink coffee without thinking about the farmers who grew it. For decades, many small producers in Africa and Latin America were paid so little that they could hardly feed their families. Fair trade was created to change this situation.

When a product carries the fair trade label, the farmers are guaranteed a minimum price, and part of the money is invested in schools, clinics and clean water for their communities. Child labour is forbidden. Critics say that fair trade products are more expensive and that the label does not always reach the poorest. However, supporters believe that consumers have the power to make business more ethical: every purchase is a choice.

  1. Are these statements true or false? a) Small producers were always well paid. b) Child labour is allowed in fair trade. c) Fair trade products can be more expensive.
  2. What are farmers guaranteed under fair trade?
  3. How is part of the money used?
  4. What do the words in bold refer to? “it” (§1) ; “their” (§2).
  5. Find in the text words closest in meaning to: “certain to receive” ; “buying”.

Mastery of language (6 pts)

  1. Turn into the passive: “Farmers grow coffee in Ethiopia.” / “The company will invest the money in schools.”
  2. Complete: It is high time consumers (choose) ethical products.
  3. Give the opposite of: ethical, fair, legal (use prefixes).

Written expression (7 pts)
