Carthage was founded by the Phoenicians on the coast of present-day Tunisia, according to tradition in the 9th century BC. Thanks to its powerful navy and its skilled merchants, it soon became the richest city of the western Mediterranean. Carthaginian traders sold purple cloth, glass and pottery, and they had built trading posts along the North African coast long before Rome became a great power.

However, the rivalry with Rome led to three long wars, known as the Punic Wars. Although the Carthaginian general Hannibal crossed the Alps with his army and won several famous battles in Italy, Carthage was finally defeated. In 146 BC, after the Romans had besieged the city for three years, they destroyed it completely.

Historians explain the fall of Carthage by several factors: military defeats, internal political divisions and the loss of its allies, such as some Numidian kings who supported Rome.
