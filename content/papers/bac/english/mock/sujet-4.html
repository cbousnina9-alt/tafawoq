For centuries, people looked at the red point in the night sky and imagined that strange creatures lived there. Today, we know that Mars, the fourth planet from the Sun, is a cold desert. Its red colour comes from iron oxide, or rust, which covers its rocks and dust.

Mars is smaller than the Earth, and its atmosphere is much thinner, so the average temperature is far below zero. A Martian day is only a little longer than ours, but a Martian year lasts almost two Earth years. The planet has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos, and the highest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons.

Since the 1960s, dozens of missions have been sent to Mars. Robots called rovers have been driven across its surface, and they have discovered that liquid water probably flowed there billions of years ago. Scientists believe that, one day, humans will be able to visit the planet. However, the journey is long and dangerous, and astronauts will have to be protected from radiation and extreme cold.
