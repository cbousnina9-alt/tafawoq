Is fair trade the answer?

Every morning, millions of people drink coffee without knowing who grew it. In many developing countries, small farmers sell their coffee beans at very low prices, while large companies make huge profits. Fair trade was created to change this situation. It guarantees farmers a minimum price for their products and supports projects such as schools and clean water in their communities. In return, farmers must respect rules on working conditions and the environment, and they must not use child labour. Critics argue that fair-trade products are more expensive and that only a small part of the extra money reaches the farmers. Nevertheless, if more consumers chose fair-trade products, companies would be pushed to behave more ethically.
