In the heart of the Algerian Sahara lies the Tassili n'Ajjer, a huge plateau of sandstone rocks shaped by wind and time. It is famous all over the world for thousands of paintings and engravings made by people who lived there thousands of years ago.

The oldest images show wild animals such as elephants, giraffes and hippos. They prove that the Sahara was once green, with rivers and lakes. Later paintings show herds of cattle, which means that the inhabitants had become herders. In more recent periods, horses and camels appeared, while the climate was becoming drier.

The site was listed as a World Heritage Site by UNESCO, and it is protected by a national park. However, the paintings are fragile. Some visitors touch them or wet them to make the colours brighter in photos, which damages them forever. Experts say that tourism must be organised carefully if we want future generations to enjoy this open-air museum.
