Every year, millions of people buy fake products without knowing it. Counterfeit goods are copies of famous brands that are sold at lower prices. They include clothes, shoes and watches, but also medicines, toys and car parts, which can be dangerous.

Counterfeiting is not a victimless crime. It harms companies that spend money on research and design, and it causes the loss of thousands of jobs. Governments also lose taxes, because fake goods are often sold illegally. Worse still, a fake medicine may contain no active ingredient at all, and a fake brake pad may fail when a driver needs it most.

To fight this problem, customs officers must check goods at borders, and companies should use labels that are difficult to copy. Consumers, too, have a role to play. If they refused to buy suspicious products, counterfeiters would lose their market. As one expert put it, "A low price is sometimes the most expensive choice you can make."
