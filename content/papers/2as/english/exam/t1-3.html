Read the text carefully then do the activities.

In many schools around the world, students are trained to become "peer mediators". When two classmates have a conflict, a mediator meets them in a quiet room. The mediator does not judge or punish anyone. Instead, he listens to both sides and helps them find a solution together.

The results are often impressive. In some schools, fights in the playground have dropped by half. Teachers say that students learn to express their feelings with words instead of violence. "If everybody learnt to listen, there would be fewer conflicts," explains Amina, a sixteen-year-old mediator.

Of course, mediation cannot solve every problem. Serious cases still need the help of adults. But it gives young people a powerful message: peace is not only the absence of war; it is something we build every day.
