Read the text carefully then do the activities.

Every year, a family of four in a big city throws away hundreds of kilos of food. Most of this waste is not old or bad; people simply buy too much, forget products at the back of the fridge, or cook more than they can eat.

Wasting food is not only a waste of money. It also wastes the water, energy and work that were needed to produce it. When food rots in landfills, it produces methane, a gas that contributes to global warming.

Fortunately, small changes can make a big difference. If families plan their meals and write a shopping list, they will buy only what they need. Leftovers can become a new meal the next day, and fruit that is too ripe can be used for juice or jam. As the old saying goes: "Waste not, want not."

Part One — A. Comprehension (7 pts)

  1. Are these statements true or false? a) Most wasted food is old or bad. b) Wasted food has an effect on the environment. c) Leftovers can be used again.
  2. Answer: a) Why do families waste food? (give two reasons) b) What gas does rotting food produce?
  3. What or who do the underlined words refer to? «it produces methane» (§2) ; «they will buy» (§3)

Part One — B. Text exploration (7 pts)

  1. Find in the text words closest in meaning to: a) luckily (§3) b) program / organise (§3)
  2. Find opposites of: a) remember ≠ … (§1) b) more ≠ …
  3. Complete with the right form of the verbs: If you (keep) bread in the freezer, it (stay) fresh longer.
  4. Classify the words according to the pronunciation of their final -ed: wasted – planned – cooked. /t/ /d/ /ɪd/

Part Two — Written expression (6 pts)

Your school is organising an awareness week about waste. Write a short paragraph (8–10 lines) giving tips to reduce food waste at home. Use the conditional type 1 and the modal should.
